# Sunroom

*for piano and a voice that barely uses itself*

**Tempo:** about 62. **Feel:** nothing arrives, everything is already there.
**Pedal:** down, changed once a bar, and late rather than early.

---

## The key

E major. Four sharps — F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯ — and you will only ever need the first three.

E major is the bright one. It is also, on a piano, the one where the hand sits highest
on the black keys and lowest on the white, which makes an open shape feel effortless.
That is why the piece is in it.

---

## The four chords

They only ever console. There is no dominant here doing any work, nothing that pulls.

```
        Emaj9         Aadd9         C♯m9          Bsus4
  R.H.  F♯4           B3            D♯4           C♯4
        D♯4           A3            B3            A3
        B3            E3            G♯3           F♯3
  L.H.  E2            A2            C♯2           B2
```

The whole right hand moves by a step or stays put. If you can find **B3 – D♯4 – F♯4**,
you can find the other three from it without looking.

Left hand alone, if that is all you have in you:

> **E2 → A2 → C♯2 → B2**

Four whole notes. That is a complete performance of this piece and it is not a lesser one.

---

## Where the notes fall

Eight eighths to a bar. The piano is never on every one of them.

```
step   1   .   2   .   3   .   4   .
       ●       ·   ●       ●
```

Bottom note on **one**, then two notes climbing through the bar, arriving nowhere in
particular. In the fullest sections it becomes a slow arpeggio up and back down; in the
quietest it is one chord and silence.

**Do not fill the gaps.** They are most of what the piece is.

---

## The eight bars that open up

Six minutes in, two chords arrive that are not in the key:

> **Gmaj9 → Dmaj9 → Emaj9 → Aadd9**

G and D are the ♭III and ♭VII — borrowed from E *minor* and dropped into a major piece.
They are the reason that section feels like a window being opened rather than a new idea.
Play them exactly as warmly as the others; the surprise does the work on its own.

```
        Gmaj9         Dmaj9
  R.H.  A3            A3
        F♯3           F♯3
        D3            D3
  L.H.  G2            D2
```

Notice the right hand does not move at all between them. Only the bass does.

---

## The tune

It is whispered on the recording, so it is written low and narrow — you can hum it
without reaching for anything.

> **B – C♯ – B | G♯ | F♯ | B – E | C♯ – E | C♯ | B**

Every note is a chord tone. There is nothing in it to get wrong.

If you would rather sing than play: mute **Voice** on the page and it is yours. The
recording keeps a whisper rather than a note on purpose, because breath sits underneath
a piano in a way that a sung tone does not.

---

## Structure

Thirteen sections of eight bars. One thing is added at a time until about two-thirds
through, and then taken away again in the same order. Nothing else happens.

---

## A few notes on playing it half-awake

- **Late is right.** If a chord lands slightly after you meant it to, that is the piece
  working. Push nothing.
- **One dynamic.** There is no loud here. The arrangement gets fuller; the playing does
  not get harder. That distinction is the entire craft of this style.
- **Leave the last chord alone.** When you get to the end, play the E and then do not
  play anything else. Let the pedal hold it until it has genuinely gone.
- **Breathe in across two bars, out across two.** At this tempo that is under four
  breaths a minute, which is slower than you will manage at first and is meant to be.
