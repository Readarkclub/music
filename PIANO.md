# Blue Hour

*for solo piano — the version you can actually play*

**Tempo:** about 56 to the quarter. Slower is better than faster.
**Pedal:** down, and change it once a bar. If in doubt, hold it longer.
**Feel:** the whole piece lives in the black keys. Let your hand find that and it half plays itself.

---

## The key

D♭ major. Five flats, which sounds like a lot and is actually the easiest key on the
instrument — your fingers sit on the black keys and your thumbs fall on the white ones.

> D♭ &nbsp; E♭ &nbsp; F &nbsp; G♭ &nbsp; A♭ &nbsp; B♭ &nbsp; C

There is no F♭ or C♭ anywhere in this. Every white note you touch is F or C.

---

## The four chords

Left hand only, one chord a bar, bottom note first:

```
  D♭maj9        B♭m9          G♭maj9        A♭9
  D♭2 A♭2       B♭1 F2        G♭1 D♭2       A♭1 E♭2
  D♭3 F3        A♭2 D♭3       G♭2 B♭2       A♭2 C3
  A♭3           F3            D♭3           G♭3
```

Or, if that is too much to read: **play the root, then the fifth, then keep going up
through the chord.** That is all the left hand ever does in this piece.

The four together, in a loop:

> **D♭maj9 → B♭m9 → G♭maj9 → A♭9**

Play that alone, four slow beats each, and you have the piece. Everything below is decoration.

---

## Section A — the left hand

Eight eighths to a bar. Low note on beat 1, then climb.

```
      1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
L.H.  D♭3 ·   A♭3 ·   F3  ·   D♭4 ·      D♭maj9
      B♭2 ·   F3  ·   D♭3 ·   A♭3 ·      B♭m9
      G♭2 ·   D♭3 ·   B♭2 ·   G♭3 ·      G♭maj9
      A♭2 ·   E♭3 ·   C3  ·   A♭3 ·      A♭9
```

Four notes a bar, on the odd eighths. Nothing faster than that yet.

## Section B — the tune comes in

Same left hand. The right hand plays the theme on top, one or two notes a bar:

```
bar   1        2        3        4        5        6        7        8
R.H.  A♭4  C5  D♭5  C5  B♭4  A♭4  A♭4     C5   E♭5 F5   E♭5 D♭5  B♭4  A♭4
chord D♭   B♭m G♭   A♭  D♭   Fm   E♭m     A♭
```

Sing it before you play it — it goes **up** for four bars and **down** for four, and that
is the entire shape of the piece:

> A♭ — C — D♭ — C — B♭ — A♭ — A♭ &nbsp;·&nbsp; C — E♭ — F — E♭ — D♭ — B♭ — A♭

Hold each note as long as it wants. The bar lines are a suggestion.

## Section C — the window

Eight bars in A major, out of nowhere. Same idea, new colour — this is the part that
should feel like a window opening.

```
chord  A    E    F♯m  D    A    F♯m  D    A♭9
R.H.   E5   B4   C♯5  D5   E5   G♯5  E5   C5
       F♯5       E5        A5   F♯5  D5
```

The last bar is the trick: **A♭9 is the door back.** Land on C5 over it and you are home
in D♭ again without having to think about it.

## Ending

Return to Section A, play it twice more, getting quieter each time, then:

```
      1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
L.H.  D♭2 ·   ·   ·   ·   ·   ·   ·      D♭maj9, pedal down
R.H.  ·   ·   ·   D♭5 ·   ·   ·   ·
```

Three times. Let each one die all the way out before the next. Then lift the pedal last,
after the sound has already gone.

---

## Structure

**A – A – B – B – C – C – B – ending.** About seven minutes at the right tempo, and it
loops cleanly if you want longer.

---

## If you are too tired to read any of this

Put your left hand on **D♭ A♭ F** and roll it slowly. Move the bottom note to **B♭**, then
**G♭**, then **A♭**, and come back. Four bars, over and over, right hand wherever it lands.
That is ninety per cent of what the recording is doing.

---

## A few notes on playing it tired

- **Let the pedal do the work.** Every chord in this piece is a stack of thirds that
  tolerates being smeared together. If your fingers are slow, the pedal covers it — and the
  blur is closer to the intended sound than a clean version would be.
- **Play it too quietly.** Quieter than feels right. Tension shows up first as weight in
  the forearm, and playing at a whisper makes that obvious enough to let go of.
- **Wrong notes are nearly free.** Almost every black key belongs to D♭ major. If you land
  somewhere unplanned you are still in the key — hold it, let it resolve down a step, and
  it will sound deliberate.
- **Breathe with the bars.** In for one, out for the next. At this tempo that lands around
  seven breaths a minute, which is roughly where your nervous system settles on its own.
  You do not have to try to relax; the tempo does it for you.
- **Drop the ninth if the reach bothers you.** D♭maj9 works perfectly well as plain D♭ —
  root, fifth, third. Same piece, two fewer fingers.
