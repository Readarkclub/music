# Evensong

*for violin — the tune, and nothing you have to fight for*

**Tempo:** about 58 to the quarter, in three. Slower is better than faster.
**Position:** first position throughout, except where marked. Nothing above the E string's B.
**Feel:** every note is a long bow. If you are changing bow more than twice a bar, slow down.

---

## The key

D major. Two sharps, and both of them fall under the fingers where the hand already
wants to be — F♯ with the second finger on the D string, C♯ with the second on the A.

The open strings are doing half the work here. **D and A are the two most common notes
in the tune**, and both are open strings. Let them ring; don't stop them with a finger
you don't need.

---

## The tune

Eight bars, one long arch. Up for four, down for four. That is the whole piece.

```
bar   1        2        3       4         5        6        7        8
      A   D    F♯  E    D       C♯   E    F♯  A    B   A    G   F♯   E
      ↑ open   2nd fing         2nd  1st  ↑        ↑        ↑
chord D        Bm       G       A         D        G        Em       A
```

On the staff, in fingering rather than notes:

```
      A string          A string          A string        A string
bar 1  0 . . 3 . .   |   .  .  .  .  .  .   |   3 . . . . .   |   2 . . 4 . .
       A     D           F♯       E             D                 C♯    E
```

Sing it before you play it:

> **A – D | F♯ – E | D | C♯ – E | F♯ – A | B – A | G – F♯ | E**

If you can hum that, your left hand already knows where to go.

---

## Where the notes sit

Everything is on the A string and the E string, first position.

| Note | String | Finger |
|---|---|---|
| A | A | open |
| B | A | 1 |
| C♯ | A | 2 |
| D | A | 3 |
| E | E | open |
| F♯ | E | 1 |
| G | E | 2 |
| A | E | 3 |
| B | E | 4 |

The one decision: **E can be the open E string or the fourth finger on the A string.**
Use the open E when the note is long and you want it to ring. Use the fourth finger when
it sits between two notes on the A string and you don't want to cross.

---

## The middle section

Eight bars in F major, out of nowhere, and then back. Same shape, one flat.

```
      C   F    D       A   D     B♭  A    C        F   D    E   G    C♯
chord F        Bb      Dm        Gm       F        Bb       C        A7
```

B♭ is the first finger on the A string, pulled back toward the nut. It should feel
*low* — if it feels normal you are playing B natural.

The last bar is the door home: **that C♯ over the A7 is the leading note back into D.**
Lean on it a little and let it resolve.

---

## Structure

**A – A – B – B – canon – canon – A – A – A – ending.** About five and a half minutes.
The recording puts the tune through a canon in the middle; you only ever play the tune.

---

## If you are too tired to read any of this

Play **A – D – F♯ – E** on the A string, four slow bows, and go round. That is the first
half of the first phrase and it works on its own against the whole recording.

---

## A few notes on playing it tired

- **Let the bow do nothing quickly.** One bow to a bar is not too slow. The piece is
  built on long strokes; short ones make it anxious, which is the opposite of the point.
- **Play it too quietly.** Quieter than feels right. Tiredness shows up as bow pressure
  before it shows up anywhere else, and a whisper makes that obvious enough to release.
- **Don't vibrate at first.** Start every note plain and let the vibrato arrive late, if
  it arrives. That is how the recording plays it, and it is easier on a tired hand.
- **Wrong notes are cheap.** The piece is D major and its close relatives. A wrong finger
  is almost always still in the key — hold it, let it fall to the note below, and it will
  sound intended.
- **Breathe with the bars.** In across two, out across the next two. At this tempo that
  lands a little under five breaths a minute, which is deep. You do not have to try to
  relax; the tempo does it for you.
