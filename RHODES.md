# Afterglow

*for keys — four chords, and the discipline not to add a fifth*

**Tempo:** about 74. **Feel:** swung sixteenths, and everything a hair late.
**Sound:** Rhodes if you have one. Anything warm with a soft attack if you don't.

---

## The loop

Four bars. That is the entire piece. It comes round twenty-four times.

> **Cm9 → A♭maj9 → E♭maj9 → B♭9sus**

In C minor those are **i – ♭VI – ♭III – ♭VII**, which is the most-used loop in this
whole genre for a reason: nothing in it wants to resolve, so it can go round forever
without ever asking you to pay attention.

---

## The voicings

One hand position. Left hand takes the bottom note, right hand the top three, and the
hand barely moves between chords — that stillness is the sound.

```
        Cm9          A♭maj9        E♭maj9        B♭9sus
  R.H.  D4           E♭4           D4            E♭4
        B♭3          A♭3           B♭3           A♭3
        E♭3          E♭3           E♭3           E♭3
  L.H.  B♭2          A♭2           B♭2           A♭2
```

Look at what actually moves: **E♭ never moves at all**, and the other three shift by a
step or not at all. If you can play the first chord you can play all four.

Left hand alone, if that is all you want:

> **B♭2 → A♭2 → B♭2 → A♭2**

---

## Where they land

Sixteen steps to a bar. The chord is **not** on the beat except the first time.

```
step   1 . . . 2 . . . 3 . . . 4 . . .
        ●     ·   ●   ·     ●   ·   ·
        1           7        11
```

Chord on **one**, then a push on the **"a" of two**, then another just before **three**.
Both pushes land *before* the beat they belong to. That anticipation is most of the groove
— play them on the beat instead and the whole thing sits up straight and stops working.

If that is too much, play the chord on beat one and nothing else. The recording does
exactly that for the first sixteen bars.

---

## The bridge

Eight bars that go somewhere warmer and come back.

> **Fm9 → D♭maj7♯11 → A♭maj9 → E♭maj9 | Fm9 → D♭maj7♯11 → B♭9sus → Cm9**

D♭maj7♯11 is the one chord worth learning properly. Play **D♭ – F – C – G**: the G is the
♯11 and it is the reason the chord sounds like light through a window rather than a
textbook. Do not add the A♭.

---

## The tune

If you want to play the melody instead, it is eight bars and stays in one octave:

> **G – B♭ | C | B♭ – G | F | G – C | E♭ – D | C – B♭ | G**

Every note is a chord tone except the D, which is the ♯11 over A♭ and is meant to sound
slightly wrong for half a bar.

---

## A few notes on playing it late

- **Behind the beat, always.** If you are ever early you are playing a different genre.
  Think of the beat as something you are catching up to, not something you are hitting.
- **Do not voice-lead cleverly.** The stillness is the point. Moving inner voices around
  makes it interesting, and interesting is the failure mode here.
- **Let the pedal blur two chords together at the change.** A quarter-second of overlap
  is not a mistake in this music, it is the reverb you do not have.
- **Play it too quietly.** As with everything else on this shelf.
- **Stop before you are bored of it.** The piece is written to stop being interesting
  after about a minute, and then to keep going for four more. That is the medicine.
